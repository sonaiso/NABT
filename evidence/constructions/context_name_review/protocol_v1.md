# Context-only proper-name agreement review

Authored after the legacy v3 regression was discovered, and frozen before the correction. This material is not a natural corpus and is not a blind holdout. The unchanged original 86-case material and all historical results remain separate. Names نِبْرَاس and رِهَام do not occur in the earlier discourse/construction gold; their identity and semantic profile are explicit context premises, not acquired lexical facts.

There are 16 cases: four positive controls, ten boundaries, and two context-replay cases. The positive controls include masculine, feminine, unmarked input, and an independently source-qualified PN. The boundaries test disagreement, missing gender/number/person kind, same-name competitors (including an unknown competitor), a contradictory independently sourced PN, an independently sourced PN with missing features, and an ordinary noun with missing lexical features. The last two cases require context substitution/withdrawal to invalidate the old certificate and a fresh read to preserve the missing or contradictory premise.

All source rows are authored QAC-shaped evidence with preserved locators, FORM/POS/lemma/root and optional gender/number. No CASE/MOOD, syntactic role, attachment or answer is provided. The known-PN and unknown-PN variants are deliberately controlled source descriptions. They do not claim lexical truth about a natural name. Context-derived person traits may condition semantic adjective agreement only on the context-only PN path, with one resolved identity and an explicit source receipt; they must not appear as newly acquired lexical gender/number or as invented subject inflection. A sourced PN or common noun is not made context-only by missing lexical features.

The scorer checks exact grounded claims and polar answers; correlated subject/predicate assignments and agreement receipts; preserved ambiguity; absence of claims under blockers; unchanged lexical profile; and certificate replay on the original and changed contexts. The report field mapping may adapt to the public schema without changing these expectations. A missing report field is a measured failure or an explicit adapter issue, never a reason to change the material.
